My Story

This is my story — the honest version. No filter, no pretending. Read it if you want to know me:

  1. [01]//hello.log

    Hey, I'm a young Iranian

    Hi. I'm Mohammad Reza — a young Iranian from Gilan. I grew up surrounded by phones and the internet, especially operating systems. Any phone I got my hands on, I loved to tinker with. But here's the weird part: I never really chased them seriously. Everything was right in front of me, but somewhere in my head a voice kept saying "later." And that "later" became years.

  2. [02]//nickname.log

    The "Zombie" nickname

    From first to sixth grade, I didn't have many friends. Always in some corner, far from everyone. There was a reason too — others called me weird and gave me the nickname "zombie". Not because of my character, not because of how I acted. Because of my body. I had allergies. Four out of seven days a week my nose ran, and a tissue in my pocket was a constant. With a puffy face and tired eyes. Well... if I were them, I'd have said the same.

  3. [03]//minecraft.exe

    Minecraft, and the start of something new

    Still, I had a few friends who had my back. Seventh grade didn't go bad either, but I stopped reaching out to people much. I decided to stay within myself.

    But around then — I was 13 — I met a game that changed everything. It was called Minecraft. From the very start it was different from other games. Unlike the rest where you just shoot and pass, this was an empty world you could shape yourself. And I fell in love with building. But the thing that really pulled me in was command blocks. I realized I could use commands to manipulate the game as if I'd built it myself. To me, that was magic.

  4. [04]//covid.era

    COVID and 8th grade

    When eighth grade started, COVID hit. The world stopped. And so did I. I burned through that time without doing a single thing. The reason was me — I was lazy. Not that I couldn't, I didn't want to. And later I realized how much of that time was wasted.

  5. [05]//piano.song

    Piano, and trembling hands

    Ninth grade, I made a decision. I told myself to go after something else beyond my usual stuff. The problem was... I was introverted, but at the same time something deep down kept screaming "look at me!" I didn't know what to do. Until one day I decided to start music. Piano.

    In three years I had three concerts — 30 people, then 100, then around 300. And all three times, behind the piano, my hands shook. The moment I was about to play, the fear of messing up had me. But each time it got a little better. My social anxiety melted away from those three concerts.

  6. [06]//command_blocks.sh

    Why Minecraft mattered so much

    Back to the real question — why was Minecraft so important to me? Because I loved showing people what I'd built. "I made this!" There was a joy there I couldn't find anywhere else. I'd learned 70% of the game's commands. I was building maps — Bed Wars, Sky Wars, TNT Run. I wanted to make something others could play in.

  7. [07]//network/

    My field: network & software

    And that's how my field of study became networking and software. From building inside a game to building something real. When I entered my program, I decided to also start learning English. I told myself, "For someone whose field is networking and software, English is a must, not a choice." So I went for it. Life was just getting serious.

  8. [08]//video_edit.mp4

    Burnt out, went back to editing

    But during my studies, something broke. Programming, in the beginning, was heavy. I got tired. I got burnt out. I didn't want to continue, so I decided to try something else. I went toward video editing — Instagram edits, clips, stuff like that. I was okay at it. But there was a problem: I had a phone, not a system. And editing on a phone, no matter how good you are, falls short somewhere. I felt like I was hitting a ceiling that wouldn't break.

  9. [09]//my_pc.desktop

    Finally got a PC

    Finally got a system. I decided to continue video editing, but... the software felt confusing. I'd worked on a phone for years and now real software felt like a spaceship.

  10. [10]//linux/test

    Linux, and a feeling of freedom

    One day I said, "Let me try something else." I went toward Linux. I installed various distros, broke them, fixed them, broke them again, fixed them again. And I learned the basics. Unlike programming which had burnt me out, Linux gave me joy. A kind of freedom.

  11. [11]//friends.mp4

    My friends, the most important thing

    But more important than anything, my friends. I made videos with my friends — both those I know virtually, and those close to me. For me, friends are more important than anything. Really. Maybe you'll be the next one?

  12. [12]//python.learning

    Now: Python

    And then, I made a decision: go back to programming. From scratch. And now I'm learning Python. There's still a road ahead, but at least I'm walking again. If you made it this far, thanks for reading my story.
